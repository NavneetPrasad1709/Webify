# Outreach templates

One rule underneath all of these. Every message shows the prospect that you
already looked at their thing. Not "I visited your website", which everyone
says. Something specific enough that you could not have sent it to anyone else.

That is the entire difference between a reply rate near zero and one that gets
you a first client this month.

---

## 1. Upwork proposal

Most proposals open with "I am a full stack developer with 5 years of
experience". The client has read forty of those. Open with their problem.

```
Hi [name],

[One specific observation about their site or brief. "Your pricing page loads in 6 seconds on mobile, which is where most of your traffic is." Or "You are asking for a redesign but the brief reads like the real problem is that nobody understands what you sell."]

Here is how I would approach it:

[Two or three lines. Concrete. Name the first thing you would do and why.]

Three sites I built this year, all live and open right now:
saas.webify.org.in
webify-dentist.vercel.app
webify-luxory-homes.vercel.app

I work fixed price, agreed before anything starts, and you get a staging URL in the first week so you are watching it get built rather than waiting.

Happy to send a quote once I understand [the one thing you genuinely need to know]. What is [that question]?

Navneet
```

Ending on a question matters. A proposal that ends with "looking forward to
hearing from you" gives the client nothing to reply to.

**Send 10 a day for the first two weeks.** Only to jobs posted within the last
24 hours, where the client has a verified payment method and a hire rate above
50%. Skip everything else no matter how good it looks.

---

## 2. LinkedIn connection request

300 characters, and most people waste them on "I'd like to add you to my
network".

```
Hi [name], I build websites and web apps for [their kind of company] and I liked what you wrote about [specific thing]. Not selling anything, just useful people to know. Navneet
```

Do not pitch here. The connection request buys you the right to send message
two, and pitching costs you both.

---

## 3. LinkedIn DM, after they accept

Wait a day. Then:

```
Thanks for connecting, [name].

I had a look at [their site] out of curiosity and noticed [one specific, honest thing: a slow page, a confusing nav, a mobile layout that breaks, copy that does not say what they do].

Not a pitch. I run a small studio that builds this kind of thing, so I notice. If it is useful I can send you a two-minute screen recording of what I would change, free, no strings. If not, ignore this and I will not follow up about it.
```

The free recording is what converts. It costs you ten minutes, it proves you
can think, and almost nobody else is doing the work.

**When they say yes:** record your screen, walk their site, name three
problems, say what you would do about each. Do not pitch at the end. Say "if
you want me to fix any of it, happy to quote." That is enough.

---

## 4. Cold email

Subject lines that get opened are short and specific. No "Quick question".

```
Subject: [their company] homepage, one thing

Hi [name],

[Specific observation. One sentence.]

I build websites for [their kind of company]. Recent work: saas.webify.org.in and webify-dentist.vercel.app, both live.

If [the problem] is worth fixing, I can quote it in a day. If it is not, no hard feelings and I will not chase.

Navneet
Webify
webify.org.in
```

Under 100 words. Every extra sentence costs you replies.

**Send from your own address, one at a time, no tracking pixels, no sequences.**
Fifty personal emails beat five hundred automated ones, and they will not get
your domain flagged as spam before you have a single client.

---

## 5. White-label partnership

The most under-used channel available to you, and the one where a new studio
gets paid work fastest. Design studios and marketing agencies constantly need a
developer and would rather not hire one. You do not need a brand for this, only
competence.

Target: small design studios, freelance designers with more work than time,
marketing agencies who sell websites but subcontract the building.

```
Hi [name],

I build in Next.js and React and I work white-label for studios that sell websites but would rather not staff the development.

Your client never hears my name. You get fixed-price quotes, a staging URL from week one, and someone who will tell you when a design will not survive contact with a browser.

Recent builds: saas.webify.org.in, webify-dentist.vercel.app

If you have anything queued that you would rather hand off, I have capacity this month.

Navneet
webify.org.in
```

One good partner can be worth more than fifty cold emails, because it repeats.

---

## Where to spend the first two weeks

| Channel | Effort | How fast it pays |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Upwork proposals | 10 a day | Fastest. Buyers already have budget and intent |
| White-label outreach | 5 studios a day | Slower to start, repeats once it lands |
| LinkedIn DMs | 10 a day | Builds while the other two run |
| Posting on LinkedIn | 3 a week | Compounds. Costs nothing but time |
| Cold email | 20 a day | Only after the above are running |

Do not start all five. Start with Upwork and white-label, because both put you
in front of people who are already trying to buy the thing you sell.
