# Upwork profile

Upwork is where the buyers already are, which is why it produces a first paid
project faster than anything else available to a studio with no track record.
It costs fees you would rather not pay. Pay them for the first few projects and
move the relationships off-platform later, legitimately, when the contract
allows.

Nothing here needs a domain email.

---

## Title

70 characters. This is what shows in search results beside your photo.

```
Web Developer | Next.js & React | Websites, SaaS Dashboards, Landing Pages
```

Not "Full Stack Developer". Every second profile says it, and it tells a buyer
with a website problem nothing about whether you solve it.

## Overview

5,000 characters, but only the first two lines show before "more". Almost
nobody clicks more, so those two lines have to do the selling.


## Rate

Set **$30/hour** to start.

This is a platform field, not a price on your website, so it does not touch the
rule about keeping amounts off webify.org.in.

Do not go to $10. On Upwork a low rate does not read as good value, it reads as
inexperience, and it attracts the clients who will argue about every hour. Do
not go to $60 either: with no reviews yet, nobody will take the risk. $30 is
credible for the work in your portfolio and leaves room to raise it after three
five-star reviews.

Most of your projects should be **fixed-price** contracts anyway. The hourly
rate is mainly a signal.

## Specialized profile

Upwork lets you add specialized profiles alongside the general one. Add
**Web Development** and **UI/UX Design**. It costs nothing and puts you in two
search categories instead of one.

## The part that actually decides it

Your profile does not win work. Proposals do. Read the proposal template in
`brand-kit/outreach/TEMPLATES.md` before you send a single one, because the
first ten proposals you send will teach a buyer more about you than anything
written above.
